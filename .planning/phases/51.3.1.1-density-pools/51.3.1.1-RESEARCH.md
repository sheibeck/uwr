### Locked Decisions

**Source design (owner, 2026-10-08)**
- **D-00:** The owner's design brief "Dynamic Density Pool" is the basis (quoted in full under Specific Ideas in CONTEXT.md). It replaces the earlier single "mob density per location" number.

**Pools vs today's enemies**
- **D-01:** **Pool only, nothing visible.** No ordinary enemies stand at a place. The pool is the truth; a fight starts only when a pull, a travel encounter or a gathering ambush draws a group from it. Owner: "Pool only, nothing visible. Nearby shows creature types with a narrative description of their density. Let's have immersion here. A pull draws from the pool."
- **D-02:** **Creature families with role members.** A family (e.g. Goblins) has 3-4 members by role (tank, damage, support/healer, caster), each an enemy type with its own narrative name; the server already has enemy roles (`ENEMY_ROLES`: tank, damage, healer, caster).
- **D-03:** **Nearby** lists each family at the place with a narrative density line (e.g. "Salt-Crust Skitterers swarm the pans.") and a Pull action on the family. No individual enemies are listed.
- **D-04:** **Density is per family, plus a place safety rating.** Each family has its own level 0-3; the place also shows a rolled-up safety rating.
- **D-05:** **Hidden count, shown as a level.** The server keeps a population number per family per place (e.g. 0-100) so kills and regrowth move it smoothly; thresholds map it to the four level words. Players only see the words.
- **D-06:** **Density lines are rule-based templates** (per level and temperament), not AI-written. The owner approves the exact wording before it ships.
- **D-07:** **Named enemies, bosses and quest targets stay individuals**, visible and pulled as today.
- **D-08:** **Safety rating = a word plus a colour by risk** (Safe / Quiet / Risky / Deadly, coloured like the con colours), from the families' density and the player's level gap; shown on the place, the exit chips and the map.

**Encounters on travel and pulls**
- **D-09:** **Travel rolls on entering and on leaving.** Arriving rolls against the destination's pools; leaving rolls against the place left.
- **D-10:** **Chance = density x temperament x level gap.** Higher density raises it; aggressive families attack, skittish ones rarely do; creatures above your level are more likely to jump you, far below you they leave you alone. Safe places never roll. Rolls are seeded (deterministic) and dialable.
- **D-11:** **Group size by density, roles by rule.** Scarce 1, Stable 1-2, Overrun 2-4 (dialable). The first member is a front-liner (tank or damage); extras add support or casters; never a group of only healers. A large level gap may trim the size.
- **D-12:** **Pull on a family draws a group sized by density.** This replaces the earlier "careful pull brings one enemy" rule (quick task 261006-a0i). A careful single pull can come back later as an ability.
- **D-13:** **The gathering ambush draws from the same pools**, so a wiped-out place is safe to gather in.
- **D-14:** **A party travelling together rolls once.** The leader's move rolls for everyone who comes along; a hit pulls in the whole travelling party (offline members never, as CR-02).
- **D-15:** **No warning or dodge step now.** The existing danger display stands in; a pre-travel warning system is a separate backlog item (999.30).

**Depletion, rebound, vacuums**
- **D-16:** **Kills deplete per kill, scaled by role.** Each kill removes population points, more for strong roles (tank, caster) than a grunt. Roughly: a Stable family drops to Scarce after a few fights and to Wiped out after a sustained hunt. Exact numbers are dials.
- **D-17:** **Shared world.** Every player's and every background hunter's kills deplete the same pools.
- **D-18:** **Recovery back to a home level.** Each family has a home level per place (set at world generation). Below it, it regrows over time; it never grows past home on its own. Overrun only comes from a vacuum surge or an event.
- **D-19:** **Regrowth in minutes** (owner's choice; close to today's respawn pace), dialable.
- **D-20:** **Vacuums: rivals in the region surge.** Families get simple relations (rival, prey, predator) at world generation. When one is wiped out at a place, a rival or predator family from the same region moves in or rises a level. If none fits, nothing happens.

**Background hunters and feedback**
- **D-21:** **Background hunters are a light simulated tick.** A scheduled server tick picks a few places per region and thins a family there, more often where it is Overrun. No visible NPC parties. Deterministic, dialable, off or low until tuned.
- **D-22:** **Feedback: description, rating and rumours.** Nearby's density lines and the safety rating update live; the place description reflects it; NPCs and the World events feed mention big shifts.
- **D-23:** **World events (Phase 52.4)** for: a family wiped out at a place, an Overrun surge, a vacuum takeover, and region-wide trends. An Overrun surge is a natural "cull the swarm" World event with contribution and rewards.
- **D-24:** **The admin dial** (following the 51.3 `/economy` pattern) tunes: encounter chance, group size by level, depletion speed, creature regrowth speed and resource regrowth speed (separately, D-28), the per-player harvest cap, and hunter activity; global, per region, by difficulty and by family. (Superseded by D-51: no dial ships in this phase.)

**Families from world generation**
- **D-25:** **World generation creates families** with 3-4 role members and narrative names; the server sets every stat (the 51.3 "AI names, server stats" rule). Existing worlds: today's enemy types are grouped into families by creature type, with missing roles filled by rule. Any prompt wording change needs the owner's approval of the exact text.

**Resources use the same pools**
- **D-26:** **Gatherables become resource pools too.** Each place keeps a pool of resource kinds (e.g. Panlight Salt, Brinewort) at a density level 0-3 with a hidden count; there are no individual nodes. Gathering draws from the pool and depletes it; it regrows to a home level over time; Nearby lists resources with a density line. To settle when planning: whether density changes yield or rarity, time-of-day and terrain availability (51.3 regional gatherables are tagged with terrain and time of day), whether background gatherers exist, and the creature ambush while gathering (D-13).
- **D-27:** **Resource pools are shared, with a per-player harvest cap.** Owner: "I actually prefer your shared pool with cap idea. I want the enforce a shared world with competition." Everyone gathers from the same resource pools at a place; each player can take only so much per place within a time window (the cap), which stops bots and hoarders from stripping a place. Creature families are shared too (D-17).
- **D-28:** **Separate regrowth for resources and creatures, each with its own dial, plus the harvest cap.** Resources regrow slowly, over hours by default; creatures regrow in minutes (D-19). Two regrowth speeds tunable independently, and a per-player harvest cap (amount and window) per place.

## Summary

Today every ordinary creature is a standing `enemy_spawn` row (3-6 per place, `getLocationSpawnCap`, `helpers/location.ts:18`) created by `spawnEnemy` (`location.ts:426`) and replaced on every day/night turn (`index.ts:300`) or 5 minutes after a kill (`ENEMY_RESPAWN_MICROS`, `reducers/combat.ts:58`). Every fight path (Nearby pull via `start_pull` -> `resolve_pull`, `start_combat`, the gather ambush, quest-item aggro, `pull_named_enemy`, event spawns) ends in `startCombatForSpawn` (`combat.ts:176`) -> `addEnemyToCombat` (`combat.ts:91`), which is the only `combat_enemy` insert. `combat_enemy.spawnId` is a plain `u64` the engine only uses to reset the spawn after a fight (`resetSpawnAfterCombat`, `helpers/combat_rewards.ts:185`) and to find event links, so pool enemies can carry `spawnId = 0n` with no other engine change. Resources are personal `resource_node` rows rolled by `performPassiveSearch` on every arrival (`helpers/search.ts:151-175`), not shared nodes at all.

The recommended design adds eight new tables (all additive; five private, one public mirror) and a handful of defaulted columns, with the hidden count in a private `place_pool` table and a public `pool_level` mirror that carries only the 0-3 level, the level range and the display strings. A new pure rules file `data/density_rules.ts` holds every tunable number as a named constant (D-51), and two more pure shared files (`data/place_rating.ts`, `data/density_lines.ts`) feed the client and server identically. Depletion is settled once per fight inside `clearCombatArtifacts` (`combat.ts:387`, the single place all end paths delete `combat_enemy` rows). Regrowth is computed lazily from elapsed time and surfaced by one self-rescheduling module-guarded `tick_pools`, which also runs the vacuum, the hunter and the one-time cursor-batched migration. Travel encounters hook into `performTravel` (`helpers/travel.ts:19`) once, which all three travel callers share.

D-53 is confirmed in code: `resolveAbility` (`helpers/combat.ts:379`) handles `heal` (L512), `shield` (L698), `taunt` (L720), `cc` (L837), `drain` (L855) and `execute` (L879) from the player's point of view; an enemy casting them heals or shields a player, stuns or drains or executes its own side, or creates an aggro row keyed by the enemy id. The enemy-side `dispatchAbility` also hard-codes `damageType: 'physical'` and `effectType: undefined` (`combat.ts:1236-1252`). Two further findings change the plan: (1) `world_gen` is tuned to `maxTokens 2560` against a measured p99 of 1988 with no auto-retry (`data/llm_tuning.ts:369`), so adding families to the same reply will be cut off and fail the stage unless the budget is raised in the same change; (2) the client has no named-enemy wiring at all today (no `pull_named_enemy` call, no `named_enemy` subscription), so the "Named & quest targets" group is new client work, and `named_enemy` rows are per character, not per place.

**Primary recommendation:** Build it as pure rules first (density constants, rolls, rating, lines), then additive schema (publish locally at once to catch a refused column), then one pool engine module used by combat, gathering, travel and one tick; route every fight through a generalised `startCombat` that takes drawn enemies, keep `enemy_spawn` only for named, quest and event enemies, and gate the prompt rewrite behind owner approval as a separate late plan.

## Research Findings by Question

### 1. Today's enemy model, end to end [VERIFIED: codebase]

**Tables** (`schema/tables.ts`): `enemy_template` L811 (public; `role`, `creatureType`, `groupMin/Max`, `level`, `maxHp`, `isBoss?`, `isSocial?`), `enemy_role_template` L839 (one row per template from world gen: `roleKey`=`role`=`'melee'|'ranged'|'caster'`), `enemy_ability` L856 (per template), `location_enemy_template` L942 (private; place -> allowed types), `enemy_spawn` L956 (public; `state` available/pulling/engaged/depleted, `groupCount`, `level` default 0), `enemy_spawn_member` L978, `pull_state` L992 + `pull_tick` L1018 (scheduled `resolve_pull`), `combat_encounter` L1052, `combat_enemy` L1092 (`spawnId` required `u64`), `enemy_respawn_tick` L528 (scheduled `respawn_enemy`), `named_enemy` L218 (public, **per character**: `characterId`, `locationId`, `isAlive`, `lastKilledAt`, `respawnMinutes` 30).

**Spawn lifecycle.**
- Create: `spawnEnemy` (`location.ts:426-551`) picks a type by level fit from `location_enemy_template`, a group size from `groupMin..groupMax` biased by region danger, then inserts `groupCount` separate `enemy_spawn` rows (each `groupCount 1`) plus one `enemy_spawn_member`. `spawnEnemyWithTemplate` (L553-645) does the same for a chosen template and **also copies `groupMin..groupMax`**, so a quest boss spawns as several copies (D-53).
- Fill: `ensureSpawnsForLocation` (L333) tops a place up to `getLocationSpawnCap` (3 + id % 4) when characters are present; `ensureLocationRuntimeBootstrap` (L367, run by admin `/synccontent`, `reducers/commands.ts:173`); `respawnLocationSpawns` (L403) deletes all available non-event spawns and respawns them, called per non-safe location from `tick_day_night` (`index.ts:296-302`).
- Respawn after a kill: `resetSpawnAfterCombat` (`helpers/combat_rewards.ts:185-249`) deletes an emptied spawn and inserts an `enemy_respawn_tick` at `now + ENEMY_RESPAWN_MICROS` (5 min); `respawn_enemy` (`combat.ts:1158`) calls `spawnEnemy` if under the cap.
- Callers of `ensureSpawnsForLocation` (all pass it through deps): `helpers/travel.ts:186`, `reducers/characters.ts:112`, `reducers/intent.ts:646,1463,1648`, `reducers/movement.ts:32`, `reducers/quests.ts:436,488`, `helpers/llm_apply.ts:551`, `helpers/world_gen.ts:351`.

### 4. Migration without `--clear-database` [VERIFIED: codebase] [CITED: 51.3-RESEARCH.md]

**Idempotent backfill = a self-rescheduling cursor tick, not an `init` reducer.** `tick_pools` is armed by `ensurePoolTickScheduled(ctx)` in `init` and `clientConnected` (exact pattern of `ensurePassageSweepScheduled`, `index.ts:439-455`: arm only when no tick row exists). 1. **Group ordinary types into families.** Ordinary template = linked from `location_enemy_template` to a non-safe location in the region AND not a boss/named template (not in `named_enemy.enemyTemplateId`, not a `boss_kill` quest target, `isBoss` not set, not a world-event `enemyTemplateKey`). 2. **Map roles and fill gaps by rule.** Existing `melee` -> first one `tank`, further `melee` -> `damage`; `ranged` -> `damage`; `caster` -> `caster`. Missing roles among `tank, damage, healer, caster` are filled with new server-made `enemy_template` rows (same level as the family's base member, stats from `enemyStatsForLevel`, one `enemy_role_template`, abilities from `memberAbilities(role)`, `creatureType` copied). Filler names come from a rule (`{last word of base singular} {Warder|Raider|Mender|Hexer}`, a `ROLE_NAME_WORD` const). `[ASSUMED]` wording, flagged for owner copy approval. A family is allowed to stay at one member when the type was quest-invented (Open Question 2); the draw function handles any member set.
3. **Family nouns.** Singular = the base member's name; plural by a tested `pluralize()` (regular English rules plus an irregular list); temperament by creature type (`beast`/`elemental` aggressive, `undead` aggressive, `humanoid` wary, `construct`/`aberration` wary), `iconKey` by creature type. `[ASSUMED]` all of it, owner review.
4. **Pools per place.** For each non-safe location linked to a migrated type, one creature `place_pool` per family: home level by the rule in Section "Home level rule", `count = homeCount(homeLevel)` (places start full), mirrored into `pool_level` with the level range from the members' `placeSpawnLevel` at that place.

6. **Retire standing state.** Delete `enemy_spawn` (+ `enemy_spawn_member`) rows at region locations where `state === 'available'` and no `event_spawn_enemy` link; delete `resource_node` rows with `state 'available'` and `lockedByCharacterId` unset. ### 5. Scheduled ticks [VERIFIED: codebase]

**Module-identity guard.** Every scheduled reducer starts with `if (ctx.sender.toHexString() !== ctx.databaseIdentity.toHexString()) return;` as its **first statement** (a forged client call must not read the db or fork a second chain). `reducers/scheduled_guard.integration.test.ts` (a) forges a client sender against a throwing db proxy, (b) asserts the module sender reaches the db, (c) asserts the module reschedules exactly one tick and a forged call none, (d) pins the guard textually within 700 chars of `scheduledReducers['<name>']`, (e) asserts every `scheduled: () => scheduledReducers['x']` in `tables.ts` is in the `SCHEDULED` list or in the `coveredElsewhere` list. **Add `tick_pools` to `SCHEDULED` and to the reschedule `cases`.**

**Reschedule first, then work** (`sweep_passages`, `index.ts:447-454`): insert the next tick row before calling the work function so a throw in the work cannot end the chain. Because a reducer that throws rolls back, isolate each pool in try/catch with a console line (the round engine's `isolateRoundStep` idea).

**Design of `tick_pools` (one table `pool_tick`, scheduled reducer `tick_pools`, 60 s cadence):**
1. Regrowth for creature pools and resource pools both use lazy settlement: each pool stores `lastSettledMicros`; `settlePool(ctx, pool, now)` adds `floor(elapsed / microsPerPoint)` points toward `homeCount` (never past it), drains an above-home count toward home at `OVERRUN_SETTLE_MICROS_PER_POINT`, and for `count == 0` waits `WIPED_RESET_MICROS` since `wipedAtMicros` then sets `WIPED_RETURN_COUNT` (D-37). Any read-modify (kill, gather, tick) settles first, so correctness never depends on the tick running on time.
2. The tick iterates only pools flagged `dirty` (count != homeCount): btree index `by_dirty` on a bool column, `.filter(true)`. This keeps the minute tick proportional to active pools, not to the world (the "cost of iterating all places" question). `[VERIFIED: indexes on bool columns are not used elsewhere in this repo; confirm in the schema plan with the recorder and a local publish]` - fall back to `by_region` scans if a bool index is refused.
3. A level change (or range change) writes the public `pool_level` mirror and emits `onPoolShift`; sub-threshold changes touch only the private row.
4. **Vacuum** (`count` hits 0): pick a surge target, deterministic by seed: (a) a family with relation `rival` or `predator` to the wiped family that already has a pool at this place (set `count = OVERRUN_SURGE_COUNT`, above home, so it later settles to Stable per D-36); else (b) such a family in the same region whose `fit` includes this place's terrain but has no pool here: create a pool at home level 2, `count = OVERRUN_SURGE_COUNT`; else nothing. One surge per wipe; the wiped family returns via the long reset.
5. **Hunter** (`HUNTER_INTERVAL_MICROS` since `pool_state.lastHunterMicros`): per region, with `HUNTER_ACTIVITY_PCT` chance (seeded per region and tick bucket), pick `HUNTER_PLACES_PER_REGION` creature pools weighted toward Overrun and subtract `HUNTER_THIN_POINTS` through the same `applyDepletion` path (so shifts, lines and hooks fire identically). Low by default (see constants). Seed = `economyRoll(poolSeed(regionId, tickBucket), index)`; no `Math.random` anywhere (confirmed none in production code).
6. Cost: the hunter step needs region -> pools: `place_pool.by_region`; regions come from `ctx.db.region.iter()` (a handful of rows).

**Existing ticks that must change:** `tick_day_night` (`index.ts:276-310`) stops calling `respawnLocationSpawns`; `respawn_enemy` and `resolve_pull` keep their registered guarded bodies but drain (their tables are scheduled and cannot be removed).

## Pool engine design (`helpers/pools.ts`, used by combat, gathering, travel, tick, migration)

- `countToLevel(count)`: 0 -> 0; `< STABLE_FLOOR` -> 1; `< OVERRUN_FLOOR` -> 2; else 3 (constants below).
- `setPoolCount(ctx, pool, newCount, cause)` is the **only** writer of `place_pool.count`: clamps 0..`COUNT_MAX`, sets `dirty = (count != homeCount && count != 0 ... )`, stamps `wipedAtMicros` on reaching 0, updates `pool_level` only on level/range change, and returns `{ from, to }` levels so callers print lines and `onPoolShift` fires. A source-pin test forbids other `place_pool.id.update` writers.
- `applyDepletion(ctx, pool, points, cause)` (kills, hunters, gathers) = `settlePool` then `setPoolCount(count - points)`; on reaching 0 -> vacuum step.
- `settlePool(ctx, pool, now)` as in Section 5.
- `settlePoolKills(ctx, combatId, enemies)`: called at the top of `clearCombatArtifacts` (`combat.ts:387`, before `combat_enemy` rows are deleted); for each enemy row with `poolId > 0n` and `currentHp === 0n`, `applyDepletion(pool, DEPLETION_BY_ROLE[role])`. `clearCombatArtifacts` is the single deletion point for victory, defeat, `end_combat` and `closeFightAfterFailure` (a second call after a failed victory finds no rows, so kills are never counted twice). **Draw does not reserve population**; two fights drawing the same pool at once is allowed and the count clamps at 0. This removes any "return survivors" logic and keeps D-17 honest.
- `drawGroup(ctx, pool/family, partyLevel, seed)`: size from `GROUP_SIZE_BY_LEVEL` with the large-gap trim, composition by the role rule (slot 0 tank|damage, extras weighted caster/healer/damage/tank, healer cap 1 up to a group of 3 and 2 for 4), members drawn **with replacement** from `family_member`, each at `placeSpawnLevel(template.level, target, offset)`. Works for any member set (a family of one yields copies of it).
- Group narration at pull (UI-SPEC): `You make some noise. Two skitterers answer.` from `NUMBER_WORDS[1..4]`, singular/plural noun.

### Pattern 1: Lazy settle plus one writer
**What:** every pool access first applies elapsed-time regrowth, then one function writes the count and mirrors the level.
**When:** kill, gather, tick, migration, hunter, event surge.
```typescript
// Source: this design; mirrors helpers/online.ts "single writer" discipline
export function setPoolCount(ctx: any, pool: any, next: bigint, now: bigint) {
  const count = next < 0n ? 0n : next > COUNT_MAX ? COUNT_MAX : next;
  const fromLevel = countToLevel(pool.count);
  const toLevel = countToLevel(count);
  const home = HOME_COUNT_BY_LEVEL[Number(pool.homeLevel)] ?? 0n;
  ctx.db.place_pool.id.update({
    ...pool,
    count,
    lastSettledMicros: now,
    // stamp the wipe time once, on the step that reaches 0; clear it otherwise
    wipedAtMicros: count === 0n ? (pool.count === 0n ? pool.wipedAtMicros : now) : 0n,
    // dirty = the tick still has work: below/above home, or waiting out the wiped reset
    dirty: count !== home,
  });
  if (fromLevel !== toLevel) mirrorLevel(ctx, pool.id, toLevel); // public pool_level, level/range/strings only
  return { fromLevel, toLevel };
}
```

```typescript
export function encounterChancePct(pools: {level: number; temperament: string; lvHi: bigint}[], partyLevel: bigint): number {
  let miss = 10000n; // basis points of "no encounter"
  for (const p of pools) {
    if (p.level <= 0) continue;
    const gap = Number(p.lvHi - partyLevel);
    const bp = BigInt(ENCOUNTER_BASE_PCT[p.level]) * BigInt(TEMPERAMENT_PCT[p.temperament] ?? 100) * BigInt(gapPct(gap)) / 100n; // percent*percent/100 -> basis points
    miss = (miss * (10000n - bp)) / 10000n;
  }
  const hit = Number(10000n - miss) / 100;
  return Math.min(ENCOUNTER_MAX_PCT, hit);
}
export const encounterHit = (seed: bigint, pct: number) => rollBelow(seed, POOL_ROLL.ENCOUNTER, 10000n) < BigInt(Math.round(pct * 100));
```

### Pattern 4: Travel hook order
`validate -> leave roll (origin non-safe) -> deduct stamina/cooldowns -> moveOne for all -> enter roll once for the party -> uncharted/world-gen`. See Section 6.

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid
- **A public row carrying the count** (even "rounded"): breaks D-05 and the privacy test; keep the mirror level-only.
- **Reserving population on draw**: forces return-survivors logic and races; settle kills at fight end instead.
- **Running regrowth only in the tick**: late or failed ticks would skew the world; settle lazily too.
- **Additive offsets on one timestamp** for rolls (correlates every roll, 51.3 Pitfall 4): use `economyRoll(seed, index)` with a fixed index table.
- **Scanning with `.iter()` in a view**: not allowed (CLAUDE.md); reducers may scan.
- **Editing `src/module_bindings`**: regenerate with `spacetime generate`.
- **Building density lines in components**: the one shared `density_lines.ts` feeds client and server text.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Bare `.optional()` column refused at publish
**What goes wrong:** publish aborts or demands a clear. **Why:** auto-migration needs a default for any new column on a populated table [CITED: 51.3-RESEARCH.md:139]. **Avoid:** `.default(0n)` / `.default('')` with sentinels (`0n` = none) on `combat_enemy`, `combat_enemy_cast`, `resource_gather`, `location`. New tables are free. **Warning:** the schema plan publishes locally first (key check) to prove it.

### Pitfall 2: `world_gen` max tokens
**What goes wrong:** the enlarged fill reply is cut off at 2560 tokens, the route never retries, the stage fails (`FILL_ERROR`). **Avoid:** raise the budget in the same change (Section 8). **Warning:** `llm_tuning.test.ts` failing or a live region fill stuck.

### Pitfall 3: Kills counted twice or never
**What goes wrong:** depletion in each death site double counts or misses DoT/pet kills. **Avoid:** settle once in `clearCombatArtifacts` from rows with `currentHp === 0n`; test victory, defeat, `end_combat` and `closeFightAfterFailure`.

### Pitfall 4: Kill quests and family members
**What goes wrong:** `updateQuestProgressForKill` matches `targetEnemyTemplateId === enemyTemplateId` (`combat.ts:505`); a quest that targets a template the migration treated as "individual" can never be completed, and a quest targeting a family by plural noun has no template. **Avoid:** keep kill-type targets as pool members (Open Question 2); the resolver in `llm_apply.ts:874-905` matches template names, which family members still are.

### Pitfall 5: Public pool rows leaking numbers
**What goes wrong:** a convenience column (`count`, `home`) on `pool_level` or `combat_enemy.poolId`-adjacent data. **Avoid:** schema privacy test with exact column lists; DOM test with numeric fixtures.

### Pitfall 6: Lines spam
**What goes wrong:** a density line per kill. **Avoid:** print only on a level change; recipients are online characters at the place; regrowth silent (B11).

### Pitfall 7: Pronoun and banned-word guards
**What goes wrong:** `pronoun_rules.test.ts` fails a template line with `${...name...}` followed by `their/its/them`; `no_ripple_word` fails any use of the banned word. **Avoid:** template variables named `plural`/`singular`; new modules added to the scan; say "World event".

### Pitfall 8: Insert-site drift
**What goes wrong:** adding a defaulted column breaks every `insert` that omits it (TypeScript requires the field; the strict recorder validates columns). **Avoid:** update the listed production inserts and the fixture helpers (`combat_fight_fixture.ts`, `test-utils.ts`) in the schema plan.

### Pitfall 9: Retiring `start_pull` / `resolve_pull`
**What goes wrong:** deleting the reducer or table breaks publish (non-empty/scheduled table) or the guard test list. **Avoid:** keep `pull_state`, `pull_tick` and `resolve_pull`, guarded, draining; `WIS_PULL_BONUS_PER_POINT` and the `pull_veil` effect (`combat.ts:997-1012`) lose their consumer - leave the constants and effect type, add a pin test so 999.4 (sneak/lull) can reuse them.

### Pitfall 10: Concurrent travel/fight race on arrival
**What goes wrong:** an ambush on arrival starts a fight for the leader while AUTO-JOIN (`travel.ts:223-271`) already joined followers into a group fight. **Avoid:** run the enter roll after all `moveOne` calls and skip it when any traveller is already in an active fight.

### Pitfall 11: Reducer cost in the hunter and density lines
**What goes wrong:** per-pool writes plus per-character line loops in one transaction. **Avoid:** dirty index, bounded hunter (`HUNTER_PLACES_PER_REGION`), lines only for online characters at the changed place.

### Pitfall 12: Test fixtures that expect standing spawns
**What goes wrong:** ~22 existing test files seed `enemy_spawn`/`resource_node` (list in grep above). **Avoid:** fights keep their `enemy_spawn` fixtures (named/event paths still use them); only tests of `spawnEnemy`, `ensureSpawnsForLocation`, `start_pull`, passive-search nodes, gather and the client Nearby/enemies models are rewritten.

## Open Questions

1. **D-50 (economy size is an admin setting) vs D-51 (no admin dial or command here).**
   - Known: `/economy` and `economy_dials` exist (51.3); D-50 was recorded before D-51; the brief says "do not plan dial tables or `/populations` commands".
   - Unclear: whether one `/economy size` form counts as a new command.
   - Recommendation: ship `economySize` as a defaulted `economy_dials` column and the constants table, and wire `/economy size` only if the owner confirms; otherwise constants + column with `'small'`.
2. **Kill-quest enemy types invented by the LLM** (`llm_apply.ts:895`): D-07 says quest targets stay individuals, but a `kill 5 X` quest needs X to be fightable repeatedly.
   - Recommendation: treat `kill`/`kill_loot` targets as pool members (family of one at that place, home Scarce); only `boss_kill` templates (and `named_enemy` rows) are individuals.
3. **Time of day for resource pools** (D-26 left open): recommend dropping it (Section 3). Needs owner nod.
4. **Cap numbers and window** (4 gathers / 30 min) and the gather yield drop from an average of 4 per node to 1-3 per gather: crafting may feel tighter until 52.5 tunes it.
5. **Enemy `cc`/`taunt`:** accept no-ops (Section 2), or schedule a player-stun consumer.
6. **Paid measurement of the new `world_gen` reply** is an owner action (live, paid); until then the tuning entry reads `insufficient_data` at a safe ceiling.
7. **Copy approval** (density lines, rating lines, feed lines, filler names, ambush grammar): recommended as one consolidated review at the end, because every maincloud publish is owner-manual and all copy lives in constants; the **prompt wording** is the only item that must be approved before it is wired (it can cost money locally).

### Known Threat Patterns

| Pattern | STRIDE | Standard Mitigation |
|---------|--------|---------------------|
| Forged scheduled-reducer call forks a tick chain | Elevation | module-identity guard first statement + guard test |
| Client sends another place's pool id or a wiped family | Tampering | validate `pool.locationId === character.locationId`, level > 0, caller owns character; refuse with `fail()` |
| Hidden count inferred from public data | Information disclosure | mirror carries level/range only; schema and DOM privacy tests; no per-kill public writes |
| Bot strips a place | Denial / abuse | per-player harvest cap per place and window; hunters low; shared regrowth |
| Hunting a family out to grief others | Tampering (game) | accepted by design (shared world, D-17); vacuum and the long reset prevent permanence |
| Roll manipulation by timing | Tampering | seeds include timestamp, leader id, place and phase; each retry costs stamina or a fight; no free reroll on a hit |
| Hostile text in AI names (markup, prompt injection into later economy prompts) | Tampering | validator strips markup characters; names rendered as text nodes only (no `v-html`); world data sanitised before later prompts |
| Overlong/huge reply or many families | DoS | caps on families (3), members (4), relations, name length; token ceiling |
| Pronoun/banned-word regressions in copy | Integrity | existing repo-wide guards extended to new modules |
