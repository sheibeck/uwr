# Phase 51.3.1.1: Density Pools (was backlog 999.29) - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-10-08
**Status:** Promoted to Phase 51.3.1.1 (owner, 2026-10-08); ready to plan after an updated mock

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Ordinary creatures stop being individual enemies standing at a place, and gatherables stop being individual nodes. Each place keeps a **pool of creature families**, each at a **density level** (Wiped out, Scarce, Stable, Overrun). Travel, pulls and gathering draw groups from these pools, kills deplete them for everyone, time and a light background-hunter simulation move them up and down, and a wiped-out family lets a rival surge in. Players read density through narrative lines, a place safety rating and World events. An admin dial tunes it.

Named enemies, bosses and quest-spawned enemies stay individuals and are out of scope here.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Source design (owner, 2026-10-08)
- **D-00:** The owner's design brief "Dynamic Density Pool" is the basis (quoted in full under Specific Ideas). It replaces the earlier single "mob density per location" number.

### Pools vs today's enemies
- **D-01:** **Pool only, nothing visible.** No ordinary enemies stand at a place. The pool is the truth; a fight starts only when a pull, a travel encounter or a gathering ambush draws a group from it. Owner: "Pool only, nothing visible. Nearby shows creature types with a narrative description of their density. Let's have immersion here. A pull draws from the pool."
- **D-02:** **Creature families with role members.** Owner: "each type has different kinds of creatures. So, Goblins exist as a type, and when you fight them, you might have goblin tanks, support, casters or dps types. They'd have sufficiently narrative names, but then when you pull you get could get one more of the type with various roles. Making combat more interesting." A family (e.g. Goblins) has 3-4 members by role (tank, damage, support/healer, caster), each an enemy type with its own narrative name; the server already has enemy roles (`ENEMY_ROLES`: tank, damage, healer, caster).
- **D-03:** **Nearby** lists each family at the place with a narrative density line (e.g. "Salt-Crust Skitterers swarm the pans.") and a Pull action on the family. No individual enemies are listed.
- **D-04:** **Density is per family, plus a place safety rating.** Each family has its own level 0-3; the place also shows a rolled-up safety rating.
- **D-05:** **Hidden count, shown as a level.** The server keeps a population number per family per place (e.g. 0-100) so kills and regrowth move it smoothly; thresholds map it to the four level words. Players only see the words.
- **D-06:** **Density lines are rule-based templates** (per level and temperament), not AI-written. The owner approves the exact wording before it ships.
- **D-07:** **Named enemies, bosses and quest targets stay individuals**, visible and pulled as today.
- **D-08:** **Safety rating = a word plus a colour by risk** (e.g. Safe / Quiet / Risky / Deadly, coloured like the con colours), from the families' density and the player's level gap; shown on the place, the exit chips and the map. The owner notes the game already shows the zone's danger relative to the character; the rating builds on that.

### Encounters on travel and pulls
- **D-09:** **Travel rolls on entering and on leaving.** Arriving rolls against the destination's pools; leaving rolls against the place left. A dense place is dangerous to get into and out of.
- **D-10:** **Chance = density x temperament x level gap.** Higher density raises it; aggressive families attack, skittish ones rarely do; creatures above your level are more likely to jump you, far below you they leave you alone. Safe places never roll. Rolls are seeded (deterministic) and dialable.
- **D-11:** **Group size by density, roles by rule.** Scarce 1, Stable 1-2, Overrun 2-4 (dialable). The first member is a front-liner (tank or damage); extras add support or casters; never a group of only healers. A large level gap may trim the size.
- **D-12:** **Pull on a family draws a group sized by density.** This replaces the earlier "careful pull brings one enemy" rule (quick task 261006-a0i). A careful single pull can come back later as an ability.
- **D-13:** **The gathering ambush draws from the same pools**, so a wiped-out place is safe to gather in.
- **D-14:** **A party travelling together rolls once.** The leader's move rolls for everyone who comes along; a hit pulls in the whole travelling party (offline members never, as CR-02).
- **D-15:** **No warning or dodge step now.** The existing danger display stands in; a pre-travel warning system is a separate backlog item (999.30).

### Depletion, rebound, vacuums
- **D-16:** **Kills deplete per kill, scaled by role.** Each kill removes population points, more for strong roles (tank, caster) than a grunt. Roughly: a Stable family drops to Scarce after a few fights and to Wiped out after a sustained hunt. Exact numbers are dials.
- **D-17:** **Shared world.** Every player's and every background hunter's kills deplete the same pools.
- **D-18:** **Recovery back to a home level.** Each family has a home level per place (set at world generation). Below it, it regrows over time; it never grows past home on its own. Overrun only comes from a vacuum surge or an event.
- **D-19:** **Regrowth in minutes** (owner's choice; close to today's respawn pace), dialable.
- **D-20:** **Vacuums: rivals in the region surge.** Families get simple relations (rival, prey, predator) at world generation. When one is wiped out at a place, a rival or predator family from the same region moves in or rises a level. If none fits, nothing happens.

### Background hunters and feedback
- **D-21:** **Background hunters are a light simulated tick.** A scheduled server tick picks a few places per region and thins a family there, more often where it is Overrun. No visible NPC parties. Deterministic, dialable, off or low until tuned.
- **D-22:** **Feedback: description, rating and rumours.** Nearby's density lines and the safety rating update live; the place description reflects it; NPCs and the World events feed mention big shifts.
- **D-23:** **World events (Phase 52.4)** for: a family wiped out at a place, an Overrun surge, a vacuum takeover, and region-wide trends. Owner: "this system would then lend itself very nicely into the world event system!" An Overrun surge is a natural "cull the swarm" World event with contribution and rewards.
- **D-24:** **The admin dial** (following the 51.3 `/economy` pattern) tunes: encounter chance, group size by level, depletion speed, creature regrowth speed and resource regrowth speed (separately, D-28), the per-player harvest cap, and hunter activity; global, per region, by difficulty and by family.

### Families from world generation
- **D-25:** **World generation creates families** with 3-4 role members and narrative names; the server sets every stat (the 51.3 "AI names, server stats" rule). Existing worlds: today's enemy types are grouped into families by creature type, with missing roles filled by rule. Any prompt wording change needs the owner's approval of the exact text.

### Resources use the same pools
- **D-26:** **Gatherables become resource pools too.** Owner, 2026-10-08: "we should do this same kind of density pattern for gatherables. Instead of individual nodes." Each place keeps a pool of resource kinds (e.g. Panlight Salt, Brinewort) at a density level 0-3 with a hidden count; there are no individual nodes. Gathering draws from the pool and depletes it; it regrows to a home level over time; Nearby lists resources with a density line. To settle when planning: whether density changes yield or rarity, time-of-day and terrain availability (51.3 regional gatherables are tagged with terrain and time of day), whether background gatherers exist, and the creature ambush while gathering (D-13).

- **D-27:** **Resource pools are shared, with a per-player harvest cap.** Owner, 2026-10-08: "I actually prefer your shared pool with cap idea. I want the enforce a shared world with competition." (This replaces the earlier per-player choice; history: the owner first leaned per player because "I don't want bots or something coming through and stealing all the resources".) Everyone gathers from the same resource pools at a place, so players compete for them; each player can take only so much per place within a time window (the cap), which stops bots and hoarders from stripping a place. Creature families are shared too (D-17).
- **D-28:** **Separate regrowth for resources and creatures, each with its own dial, plus the harvest cap.** Resources regrow slowly, over hours by default; creatures regrow in minutes (D-19). The dial has two regrowth speeds (creature regrowth, resource regrowth) that can be tuned up or down independently, and a per-player harvest cap (amount and window) per place.

## Specific Ideas

The owner's design brief (pasted 2026-10-08, verbatim):

> **Objective.** Replace our static, coordinate-based creature placement ("X creatures at Y location") with a state-driven Dynamic Density Pool system. This system abstracts creature populations across travel zones, allowing populations to wax and wane based on player actions, background NPC activity, and respawn cycles. Our goal is to shift travel from a "free traversal" model to a tactical choice where zone safety dynamically reacts to the world's shared ecosystem.
>
> **1. The Core Framework: Location Pools.** Instead of tracking individual spawn coordinates, each travel zone manages an abstract pool of resident Creature Types. Within that location, each type is assigned a shifting Density Level rather than an absolute numerical count. Density acts as the primary modifier for danger, travel risk, and probability:
> - Level 3: Overrun / Swarming – The area is choked with this creature type. Travel is highly dangerous, and the chance of a random encounter is at its peak.
> - Level 2: Stable / Populated – The standard native population level. Standard risk profile during travel.
> - Level 1: Depleted / Scarce – The population has been heavily hunted or driven into deep hiding. Low encounter probability.
> - Level 0: Extirpated / Wiped Out – The creature type is completely unavailable in this zone. No encounters with this creature can be triggered.
>
> **2. The Core Loop: The "Pull" and Depletion.** When a party interacts with a zone, they dynamically draw from and alter the population pool: (1) The Encounter: When a random encounter or hunt is triggered, the system pulls a specific Creature Type from the available pool based on its current Density. (2) The Depletion: When creatures are successfully defeated within that zone, the Density Level for that specific Creature Type is reduced. (3) The Narrative Feedback: The zone's text descriptions, safety ratings, or rumors update globally to reflect the shifting environment (e.g., a zone shifting from Overrun to Scarce).
>
> **3. Shared World Simulation (Multi-Party Impact).** To simulate a living world where other parties are active in the same space: Simulated Competition: Both the Player Party and automated background NPC parties (such as rival adventurers, guilds, or hunters) pull from the exact same Creature Type pools. Shared Depletion: If background factions are heavily hunting a specific creature type in a zone, the density drops for the player too. Players might arrive at a notoriously dangerous zone only to find it already cleared out by rivals.
>
> **4. The Respawn: Ecological Rebound.** Populations are not permanently depleted; they wax and wane organically over time: Time-Based Recovery: After enough time passes without interference, a depleted Creature Type's density increments back up, simulating breeding cycles or migration. Ecological Vacuums: Wiping out one Creature Type (Density 0) can cause a narrative and mechanical shift, allowing a rival or prey creature type's density to suddenly surge to fill the void.
>
> **Design Benefits.** Strategic Travel: High-level zones are no longer hard gates. Players can choose to wait for rival parties to thin out a dangerous area, or actively hunt a route themselves to make future travel safer. Living World Feel: The world reacts globally to the actions of both the player and the environment's internal actors, making zones feel dynamic and alive.

Earlier owner framing (2026-10-08): "this could be as simple as MobDensity is a property of a location. The more dense the mobs, the more chance you have to A. get attacked when you enter or leave B. pull a group of enemies instead of a single enemy when fighting".
