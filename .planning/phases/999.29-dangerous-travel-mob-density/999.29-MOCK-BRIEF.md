# Design brief: Living places (density pools) for Unwritten Realms

Use this to update the existing UWR mocks (Ledger / Nearby rail, Map, Combat, World events). Same design system as today: Nocturne tokens, Inter, Phosphor icons, dark UI, desktop at 1280 and mobile at 390x844.

## What changes in the game

Places no longer have individual enemies or gathering nodes standing in them. Each place has **populations**:

- **Creature families**, for example *Goblins* or *Salt-Crust Skitterers*. Each family has a few members with roles and their own names, for example *Goblin Shieldbearer* (tank), *Goblin Cutter* (damage), *Goblin Hexer* (caster), *Goblin Mender* (support).
- **Resources**, for example *Panlight Salt* or *Brinewort*.

Each family and each resource has a **density** at that place, shown as a word:

| Level | Creatures | Resources |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **Overrun** (swarming, very dangerous) | **Abundant** |
| 2 | **Stable** (the normal population) | **Plentiful** |
| 1 | **Scarce** (hunted down, rare encounters) | **Sparse** |
| 0 | **Wiped out** (none here now) | **Exhausted** |

Densities change for everyone in the world:
- Kills and gathering lower them.
- Time brings them back to the place's normal level.
- Rival hunters (unseen) also thin them out.
- When a family is wiped out, a rival family can move in and surge.

## Screens to design or update

### 1. Nearby (right rail on desktop, the Here tab in the Map sheet on mobile)
- A **Creatures** section: one row per family.
  - The family name, then a short narrative density line in the place's voice, for example "Goblins swarm the old mill." or "A few skitterers still pick at the pans."
  - A small density badge with the level word.
  - A level and difficulty colour, using the existing con colours.
  - A **Pull** button. Pulling draws a group: Scarce brings 1 enemy, Stable 1-2, Overrun 2-4, with mixed roles.
  - No individual enemies are listed.
- A **Resources** section: one row per resource.
  - The name, a narrative density line ("Salt crusts thick along every pan.") and a density badge.
  - A **Gather** button.
- **Named enemies, bosses and quest targets** still appear as individuals in their own small section, as today.
- **Empty states:** "Nothing hunts here now." and "The pans have been picked clean."

### 2. Place header and exits
- The place header shows a **safety rating** word with a colour: **Safe**, **Quiet**, **Risky** or **Deadly**. It comes from the families' density and the player's level.
- The **exit chips** to neighbouring places show each destination's rating, so the player can judge a trip before taking it.
- Both **entering and leaving** a dangerous place can start a fight.

### 3. Travel ambush in the feed
- When travel triggers a fight, a narrative line appears, for example "As you cross into Glass Orchard, three goblins break from the trees!"
- Then the Encounter screen opens with the drawn group, for example a Goblin Shieldbearer, a Goblin Cutter and a Goblin Hexer.
- Show how a group of 2-4 mixed-role enemies looks on the Combat screen. Each enemy card shows its role, and its current target if there's room.

### 4. Map
- Each place node can show its safety rating as a coloured ring or dot, with a legend entry.
- Optional: a small icon on places that are Overrun.

### 5. World events
- Event cards for population shifts:
  - "Goblins swarm the Glass Orchard": an Overrun surge with a "cull the swarm" goal, contribution and rewards.
  - "The Salt-Crust Skitterers are gone from Mother Pan Undercroft": a family wiped out.
  - "With the wisps gone, gloam-rats move in": a takeover.
  - A region-wide trend, for example "Kesterlane Basin grows wilder".

### 6. Admin panel (optional, for later)
- A "Populations" panel next to the Economy panel, with settings for:
  - encounter chance;
  - group size at each density;
  - depletion and regrowth speed;
  - rival hunter activity.

## Tone
Immersive and narrative: the density lines read like the Keeper describing the scene in the second person ("you"), dry and evocative, never like numbers. Numbers stay hidden. Players only see the words and colours.

## Out of scope for the mock
- A pre-travel warning or "travel carefully" choice (a later idea).
- Visible rival adventuring parties.
